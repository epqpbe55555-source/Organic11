The core rules

No lecture, just what carries the marks.

What COOH actually is

Carbonyl (C=O) + hydroxyl (OH). The OH loses H⁺ or gets replaced, but only because the C=O next to it makes that possible.

Acidity is the defining property

Carboxylic acids are more acidic than any other class of organic compound — a real equilibrium exists in water.

RCOOH + H₂O ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H₃O⁺

Ka measures the strength

Bigger Ka → more ionization → stronger acid. Used to compare acids exactly.

Ka = [RCOO⁻][H₃O⁺] / [RCOOH]

Carboxylate is a weak base

RCOO⁻ pulls protons from water — this is hydrolysis, and it raises OH⁻ concentration.

RCOO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ RCOOH + OH⁻

The +I effect of alkyl groups

Alkyl groups push electron density onto oxygen, so releasing H⁺ is harder. That's why alcohol is a weaker acid than water.

One skeleton, three derivatives

Every functional derivative comes from swapping the OH for something else.

R-COOH → R-CO-Z (Z = -Cl, -OR', -NH₂)

Orders worth memorizing cold

ACIDITYRCOOH > HOH > ROH > HC≡CH > NH₃ > RH
BASICITYRCOO⁻ < HO⁻ < RO⁻ < HC≡C⁻ < NH₂⁻ < R⁻
ESTERIFYCH₃OH > 1° > 2° (> 3°) — alcohol side
ESTERIFYHCOOH > CH₃COOH > RCH₂COOH > R₂CHCOOH > R₃CCOOH — acid side
